ARTICLE 01 / CAMERA

The language of the lens.

Camera techniques are more than just ways of recording a subject. They influence what the audience notices, how they interpret a character and the emotions they associate with a particular scene. A close-up, for example, can emphasise a character's emotional response, while a long shot can establish their relationship with the surrounding environment.

Composition also plays an important role. Leading lines can direct the audience's attention, while negative space can create isolation or tension. Every decision within the frame contributes to the overall visual narrative.

ARTICLE 02 / MISE-EN-SCÈNE

Everything in its place.

Mise-en-scène refers to everything placed within the frame. This includes setting, costume, props, lighting and colour. Together, these elements establish the atmosphere of a scene and provide the audience with visual information about its characters and setting.

For instance, low-key lighting can create an atmosphere of mystery, while contrasting colours can draw attention to a particular subject. Even seemingly insignificant objects can carry symbolic meaning.

ARTICLE 03 / SOUND

What we hear matters.

Sound is an essential part of how audiences experience moving images. Dialogue, sound effects, ambience and music can establish setting, communicate emotions and create anticipation.

Diegetic sound originates from within the world of the narrative, whereas non-diegetic sound exists outside it, such as a background musical score. The contrast between sound and silence can also influence the audience's interpretation of a moment.

ARTICLE 04 / EDITING

It's all in the cut.

Editing determines how individual shots are arranged to construct a coherent sequence. Continuity editing maintains a sense of spatial and temporal consistency, while montage can condense time and communicate information through a sequence of images.

The duration of a shot also affects pacing. Rapid cuts can create urgency and excitement, whereas longer takes allow the audience to observe the scene and absorb its atmosphere.
